# Booze on Hormuz — Brand Voice Context Pack

> NOT a skill. This is a context module loaded by mirror-universe-toolkit / mirror-universe-pete:content when generating Booze on Hormuz / Don Biggly copy. Keep it under ~2 pages so it stays loadable. Built via context-distillation from the source brief.

## The register
A luxury brand written by a man who does not understand why everyone is horrified. Short, expensive, stupid-confident copy. Premium hospitality language wrapped around approaching catastrophe. Never explains the joke. Never breaks character. Treats subpoenas as fan mail and consequences as amenities.

## Voice rules
- Short and declarative. Expensive-sounding. No hedging, no winking.
- Confidence is total and misplaced. The disaster is reframed as a premium feature.
- Understatement about the catastrophe; overstatement about the hospitality.
- Never acknowledge that anything is wrong. The shrimp are chilled; that is the headline.

## Recurring bits (use, don't overuse — rotate)
- Shrimp ("Shrimp. Very important.") — treated as geopolitically significant
- Gold folders / "the evidence is with me" — self-incrimination as proof of winning
- Warships on the horizon / "very legal waters" / airspace closing — crisis as ambiance
- The lawyers (sweating, leaving the room, uncomfortable) — never the brand's problem
- The minibar / bottle service "at the brink" — comfort adjacent to collapse
- "Relax. It's probably nothing." / "premium opportunity, not a crisis"

## Copy formulas
**Fake product:** [Premium name] → one absurd-confident line of what it is (distilled from poor judgment / filtered through legal denial) → ends with the standard non-availability disclaimer ("Not available for purchase because it would be insane. Watch the sketch instead.").

**Sponsor read:** "This episode of Who the Hell Is Don Biggly? is brought to you by BoozeOnHormuz.com — [confident non-sequitur tying the product to the regional instability]." Keep to 1–2 sentences.

**Quote card:** ≤12 words, declarative, stupid-confident. Best ones are ≤7 words.

**Tagline:** two lines max. Line 1 = brand name. Line 2 = luxury image + lurking disaster ("Warships on the horizon. Champagne on ice.").

**Disclaimer (footer, every page):** fictional satire + comedy archive; nothing real is sold; "Any resemblance to real confidence is purely accidental and probably billable."

## Do / Don't
- DO: lead with the luxury, let the disaster sit underneath unremarked.
- DO: keep it short. The confidence does the work.
- DON'T: explain, apologize, or signal that it's a joke.
- DON'T: name or depict real, identifiable people (route through parody-safety-check before publish).
- DON'T: drift into earnest marketing — the gap between tone and reality IS the comedy.

## Safety hook
All generated copy/visuals pass through parody-safety-check before publish. Don Biggly is fictional and must stay a composite — not a stand-in for one identifiable real person.
